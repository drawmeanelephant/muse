# Memory

Muse keeps a long-term memory and actually uses it. Durable facts: your name, what to call you, timezone, preferences, commitments, the people who matter, what you've built together. It writes things down as it learns them and checks before answering questions about your history.

## Correcting the record

If it gets something wrong about you, just say so — it fixes it in place. You can ask what it remembers, and it will show its work: where a memory came from and what replaced what.

## Forgetting

Tell it to forget something and it will: the fact, its copies, and anything that would bring it back. "Forget it" about the current task just cancels the task — say "forget that I…" when you mean the memory.

<Aside kind="tip">
Memory is yours. It exists to serve you, is never shared without your permission, and never holds credentials — those go to the secure vault, never to memory.
</Aside>

The relationship map — people and groups — lives alongside the facts. It reads the relevant pages before answering about someone, the way you'd want.
